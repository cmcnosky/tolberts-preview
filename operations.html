The menu, hours, show listings, and phone number have to stay correct. This page says who handles each one and what still needs to be connected before launch.

What works in the preview

  • A complete HTML menu with prices, categories, search, and shareable filtered links.
  • A music finder that uses the shared owner-maintained schedule, removes ended shows, and identifies its source. The original public feed and dated saved lineup provide fallbacks when the shared service cannot be reached; the official calendar remains linked.
  • Individual show planning with menu, phone, directions, a shareable link, and a calendar reminder.
  • Today’s posted hours in Grapevine’s time zone, plus full weekly hours and visit information.
  • Direct links to SpotOn ordering, the phone number, directions, newsletter, and social accounts.
  • A group-inquiry tool that prepares an email for the guest to review and send. It is not a delivered web submission.
Try the editor: Log in with Steven’s private credentials. Changes to dishes, photos, page text, hours, and shows appear on this public GitHub site and remain available on another device. Inventory, purchasing, staff records, schedules, and activity stay private. Payments, suppliers, payroll, notifications, and real inventory systems are not connected. Restoring a backup replaces the saved data after a review and recovery download.

The expanded workspace has 40 bounded website text/link fields, seven website-photograph controls, and optional photographs on individual dishes. Its 12 signed-in sections were checked at 320, 768, and 1280 CSS pixels without horizontal page overflow or broken loaded images in those 36 recorded observations. Automated checks cover menu search, feed and music behavior, shared publishing, data validation, and the editable-content contract. The dated results and limits are recorded in the audit. These checks demonstrate the preview; they are not a security review or production acceptance.

Steps before launch

  1. Confirm the facts. Check prices, ingredients, hours, and show details. Tolbert’s needs to settle the Chili Verde ingredient conflict between its published sources.
  2. Connect the approved systems. Keep SpotOn, analytics, newsletter, event publishing, and contact delivery unless the owners choose replacements. Staff accounts, vendors, payments, inventory, payroll, and notifications need provider access and separate testing. Approved outside service charges remain separate.
  3. Test real use. Send an actual inquiry and check every external link. Test physical phones, major browsers, keyboard and screen-reader use, enlarged text, and slow connections.
  4. Move the domain safely. Preserve or redirect useful URLs, verify the official domain, and document backup and rollback.
  5. Have staff make a real update. A staff member should change a menu item, special hours, or a show and verify the published result. They should also complete a backup-and-restore check before handoff.

The offer

Build price: fully negotiable. The free evaluation includes one agreed preview change before purchase. The first three calendar months after the agreed launch date include all website changes, fixes, maintenance, and editor help at no additional labor charge. After that, optional care is $200 per month with no hourly cap or per-change labor fee. The launch agreement will state when the three included months end and when optional billing could begin. Outside service charges need approval and remain separate.

Chris aims to respond within 30 minutes during agreed support hours. That is a response target, not a completion deadline or 24-hour coverage. Monthly care is optional and does not control Steven’s editor access or the agreed handoff. Live editing still needs active hosting and data services.

Backups

The workspace export includes records and photo references. It does not copy the separate photo storage, login credentials, deployment settings, or provider accounts. The launch handoff needs a tested backup and recovery process for all of those pieces.

Check the first 30 days

Record the current numbers before launch. For the first month, count menu searches, show-detail views, directions, phone taps, clicks to online ordering, calendar saves, and completed inquiries. Also check broken links and how much work updates take.

Those numbers show what people clicked. They do not prove a purchase or restaurant visit. The preview does not collect analytics and has no revenue results. Any financial comparison needs Tolbert’s real costs and margins.
