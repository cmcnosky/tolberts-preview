What changed, and why it is useful

  • A restaurant-specific opening. The Main Street sign and stage photographs, Tolbert’s logo, 1976/2006 history, and daily artist panel establish the food and music identity. Menu, music, posted hours, and a seating telephone are available near the top.
  • A usable web menu. All 71 entries in eight categories are readable HTML, with search, categories, prices, reset controls, shareable query states, and the original PDF. Menu search preserves boundaries between item fields, preventing false matches such as “Tail” plus “Served” becoming “Tails.”
  • A night-out planning tool. A dedicated music page offers Tonight, the current or next Friday–Sunday weekend, a date selector, and artist/style search. Each show links directly to its details, menu, directions, seating phone number, sharing, and a calendar file.
  • Honest calendar states. The page uses the shared published show schedule, hides ended shows, and labels its source. If the shared service is unavailable, the original public feed and dated saved lineup provide the fallback. An official-calendar link remains available. It does not claim a table, ticket, current cover charge, or guaranteed availability.
  • Clear mobile actions. Menu, Music, Visit, and Order remain in the bottom action bar. The Visit section gathers weekly hours, directions, parking, calling, and common questions.

What remains before an official launch

The preview is publicly accessible and marked noindex. It has not replaced the restaurant’s domain or systems. Authenticated management edits and restored backups now update the shared GitHub website. This does not replace the restaurant’s official domain or connect its other operating systems. Menu details need owner approval, including the Chili Verde ingredient disagreement between the PDF and ordering service. Physical phones, other browser engines, screen-reader journeys, representative network conditions, actual orders, delivered inquiries, completed reservations, vendor or payment connections, real inventory, payroll, and staff notifications have not been verified or connected here. A 30-day measurement plan is proposed; no analytics collector or conversion experiment has been installed in this preview.

The working design supports a specific claim: it provides more direct, testable ways to complete the examined guest tasks and demonstrates bounded management workflows. Whether Tolbert’s guests prefer it, whether the proposed internal workflows fit the restaurant, and whether it earns incremental revenue require owner review, real users, and post-launch evidence.
